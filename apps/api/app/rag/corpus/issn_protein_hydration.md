The International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) provides evidence-backed guidance on protein intake, nutrient distribution, and hydration mechanics to support muscular hypertrophy, tissue repair, and athletic performance.

### Protein Intake Guidelines for Exercising Individuals
1. **Total Daily Intake**:
   - For individuals engaging in resistance training aimed at maximizing muscle protein synthesis (MPS) and hypertrophy, an overall daily protein intake in the range of 1.4 to 2.0 grams per kilogram of body weight per day (g/kg/day) is sufficient and evidence-based.
   - During caloric deficit or intense training blocks, higher intakes (2.3 to 3.1 g/kg of fat-free mass/day) may be required to prevent the breakdown of lean skeletal muscle tissue.

2. **Per-Meal Distribution**:
   - Optimal muscle protein synthesis is achieved when protein intake is evenly spaced throughout the waking day, approximately every 3 to 4 hours.
   - Each meal should provide approximately 0.25 to 0.40 grams of high-quality protein per kilogram of body weight, or an absolute dose of roughly 20 to 40 grams.
   - Meals should contain 700 to 3000 mg of leucine (an essential branched-chain amino acid) to effectively trigger the intracellular mTORC1 pathway that initiates translation and muscle protein synthesis.

3. **Pre- and Post-Workout Nutrient Timing ("Anabolic Window")**:
   - Consuming protein and carbohydrates either before or within 2 hours following a workout provides amino acid substrate to accelerate glycogen re-synthesis and net muscle protein accrual. The traditional concept of a narrow "30-minute anabolic window" has been revised: total daily protein intake and consistent per-meal distribution over 24 hours is far more critical than immediate post-workout ingestion.

### Athletic Hydration Mechanics
- Dehydration exceeding 2% of total body mass significantly degrades aerobic endurance, muscular power, cognitive motor control, and thermoregulatory efficiency.
- Athletes should consume 500 to 600 mL of water 2 to 3 hours prior to exercise, and drink 200 to 300 mL every 15 to 20 minutes during prolonged exertion. For sessions exceeding 60 minutes in hot or humid environments, electrolytes (sodium 300–600 mg/L, potassium) should be co-ingested to maintain plasma volume and avoid hyponatremia.
