# Title: NSCA Position Statement on Youth Resistance Training and Athletic Development
# Source: National Strength and Conditioning Association (NSCA)
# URL: https://www.nsca.com/education/articles/position-statements/youth-resistance-training/
# License: Educational Reference / Public Position Statement
# Category: youth_training
# Target Age Groups: minor_under_18

The National Strength and Conditioning Association (NSCA), in coordination with international sports medicine organizations, confirms that comprehensive, supervised youth resistance training is safe, highly effective, and beneficial for children and adolescents when structured with qualified adult supervision, age-appropriate progression, and realistic expectations.

### Safety Invariants for Athletes Under 18
1. **Dispelling the Growth Plate Myth**:
   Evidence indicates that qualified youth resistance training does not damage epiphyses (growth plates), nor does it stunt stature growth. Most youth training injuries occur due to lack of adult supervision, inappropriate equipment, excessive loading (e.g., attempting 1RM lifts prematurely), or poor motor mechanics.

2. **Prohibition of Max-Effort 1RM Testing**:
   - Maximal 1-repetition maximum (1RM) lifting is strictly contraindicated for novice young athletes. Musculoskeletal structures, tendinous junctions, and epiphyseal zones are still undergoing active ossification.
   - Training programs should prescribe multi-repetition schemes: 10 to 15 repetitions with light to moderate resistance (RPE 6 to 7), focusing strictly on motor patterning, bodyweight control, and movement execution before adding external load.

3. **Curricular Priority on Fundamental Movement Skills**:
   Youth athletic conditioning must prioritize fundamental movement skills: squatting, hinging, lunging, pushing, pulling, crawling, rotating, and jumping. Play-based conditioning, multi-directional agility games, and bodyweight exercises (push-ups, bodyweight squats, plank variations, bear crawls) form the essential neurological base for long-term athletic development (LTAD).

4. **Strict Prohibition of Caloric Restriction**:
   Adolescent and youth athletes require adequate caloric and macronutrient intake to support normal physiological growth, sexual maturation, bone accretion, and hormonal development. Dieting, rapid weight cuts, and calorie-deficit restrictions are hazardous, elevating the risk of Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (RED-S), menstrual dysfunction, and eating pathology.
