The National Institute on Aging (NIA) and the National Institutes of Health (NIH) advocate four distinct pillars of exercise for older adults (65 years and older) to prevent sarcopenia (age-related muscle wasting), preserve cognitive acuity, enhance joint mobility, and eliminate falls: endurance, strength, balance, and flexibility.

### The Four Pillars of Senior Athletic Training
1. **Balance & Fall Prevention**:
   Falls represent the leading cause of fatal and non-fatal injuries in adults over 65. Balance training should be integrated into every training session. Recommended exercises include:
   - Single-leg stands (holding a sturdy chair for safety)
   - Heel-to-toe walking (tandem walking)
   - Clock stepping and side stepping
   - Tai Chi and dynamic weight-shifting drills

2. **Mitigating Sarcopenia Through Low-Impact Resistance Training**:
   Muscle mass and isometric force output decline by approximately 1% to 2% annually after age 50 without intervention. Resistance training provides the stimulus to reverse muscle loss. 
   - Modality: Cable machines, resistance bands, dumbbells, and supported bodyweight exercises (e.g., chair squats, wall push-ups) are preferable over heavy unsupported free-weight axial loads.
   - Prescription: 2 to 3 sets of 10 to 12 repetitions per exercise, using a 2-second concentric and 3-second controlled eccentric tempo. Avoid sudden explosive ballistic movements that shock unconditioned joints.

3. **Flexibility & Joint Mobility**:
   Aging tendons and joint capsules lose elastomeric compliance. Daily stretching focused on the calves, hamstrings, hip flexors, shoulders, and chest maintains range of motion needed for functional daily living (e.g., getting in and out of chairs, climbing stairs, reaching overhead). Stretches should be held static for 15 to 30 seconds without ballistic bouncing.

4. **Cardiovascular Endurance**:
   Low-impact aerobic modalities—such as brisk walking, aquatic aerobics, recumbent cycling, and rowing—promote vascular elasticity, cerebral perfusion, and mitochondrial enzyme activity without imposing harsh ground reaction forces on arthritic knees or hips. Target 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity weekly.
